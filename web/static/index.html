When metadata indicates one of these origins, the file is reported as tier U — origin indicated, watermark unchecked — rather than pretending to have read anything.

Approaches deliberately not used

Other “AI image detectors” classify the pixels — error-level analysis, noise/PRNU residuals, frequency artefacts, or a CNN trained on GAN fingerprints. None of that is here, on purpose. Those produce a probability whose error rate shifts with every model release, they are defeated by resizing and re-compression, and they cannot tell you which tool made the file. This reports evidence you can point at in an argument instead.

The same applies to text: a perplexity-based classifier such as Binoculars is far stronger than the stylometry here (>90% detection at 0.01% false positives, versus roughly 28% at 0.8% measured on our own corpus) — but it needs two local LLMs and a GPU. The stylometry in this tool is triage, not evidence.

Not implemented
  • Video frames — container-level C2PA in MP4 is verified; picture content is not examined.
  • Audio — not examined.
  • CRL revocation — OCSP is implemented (stapled by default, live opt-in).
  • RFC 3161 timestamps are shown but their signatures are not validated.
  • c2pa.hash.collection bindings. hash.data, hash.bmff v1–v3 and hash.boxes are all implemented.
  • Encrypted PDFs are not decrypted; nested containers are followed one level deep.

What this cannot do

It cannot read Google SynthID, Meta's watermark, or the statistical text watermarks now carried by Claude and Gemini output (SynthID-Text, driven by EU AI Act Article 50, in force 2 August 2026). Those bias word choice using a private key rather than inserting anything into the file — there is nothing local to find, and verification requires the vendor's own detection API.

Absence of evidence is not evidence of human authorship. A screenshot, a re-save or a metadata strip removes every signal above while changing nothing a viewer can see. A clean result means “no markers survived”, never “a human made this”.
